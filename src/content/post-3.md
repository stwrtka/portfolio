**INTRODUCTION** <br>
Similar software but completely different user experiences. In this two-part story, I aim to explore the concept of navigating familiar environments with unfamiliar tools. Along with the importance of these unfamiliar tools having the predominant user base in mind. This story uses a version of Space Invaders as the familiar aspect. The purpose of the game is to get a high score before the time runs out. You can move around and shoot enemies to get points. Additionally, this version is based on my beliefs about what makes a game easy and fun. Originally, I was going to make Pac-Man that uses cellular automata to create the game's hallways. However, I quickly realized that I need to do more research on cellular automata. So I settled with Space Invaders. In the context of Kid Pix, drawing in general is something that is familiar to many children, but computers aren't as familiar. Children have to navigate a familiar concept with tools they barely know. Following Craig Hickman’s wish for Kid Pix where, “[n]o manual should be needed and program features should "explain themselves" through use.” There will be no guidebook/manual for this assignment and the next assignment. I want you to place yourself in the shoes of a kid staring at a screen that has something you recognize but don't know how to use.

A familiar setting with an unfamiliar device. There are no instructions, just try what feels right. <br><br>

**DATA REPERSENTED** <br>
During my map research, I tested a lot of the programs mentioned, and one that stood out to me the most was Delta Drawing. Trying to use this drawing program was very unintuitive, but it made me want to look into this user experience (UX) data more. This story builds on the idea from the previous blog and investigates how physical tools shape a person's experience with something they already know. I chose to represent this data due to the experience I mentioned previously. Despite it appearing to be familiar, the tools weren't, and I had to look up a guide to use the program. Both parts of this story use the same type of data, but not the same quantity or quality. In part one of this story, the “unfamiliar” environment will be a computer, and in part two, it will be a 3DS. The reason for not using the same physical device is to keep the concept of unfamiliar tools present in the story.<br><br>

**CREATION PROCESS: INFORMATION** <br>
The creation process of this story was more difficult compared to the map or timeline because of the amount of freedom we had in how the story was told. Creating an immersive story that depends on the reader's experience is hard. Additionally, I struggled a lot with figuring out how to incorporate the 15 important aspects. Resulting in my research leaning into user experience (UX) and user interface (UI), since user-based software programs typically have it. However, I really enjoyed the creative liberty with the tools I could use to tell my story. Naturally, I chose something programming-related to make the creation process less intimidating. Despite the tool being less intimidating, figuring out what to make with it is a lot harder compared to the map and timeline. Since I wanted to try telling an experience-based story that starts off with only my perspective, it somewhat resulted in it feeling a bit unstructured (in my opinion).The information in this assignment leaned into user experience (UX) and was very experimental. Compared to the map and timeline assignment that followed a standard narrative and were structured. The map focused on the programs before Kid Pix and Kid Pix’s UI. The timeline was very rigid in telling the exact chronological history of Kid Pix.<br><br>

**CREATION PROCESS: STORYTELLING TECHNIQUES**<br>
The storytelling technique slowly became less linear and more user-based across each assignment. The timeline focused on facts about Kid Pix and didn’t explore other possible events that have impacted Kid Pix’s history. The map steered away from retelling the facts and used these facts to explore an important aspect of drawing programs, UI. However, they both followed a linear narrative-driven storytelling style.In this assignment, I use a user-based storytelling technique via immersive storytelling. Leaning heavily away from retelling facts and into making the user shape the story based on their experience of the game.<br><br>
